Reanimator

Reanimator is an archtype that is usually used with commanders/decks that have black in its color identity. How reanimator works is that when your creatures die they go into the graveyard, many commanders or decks that run reanimator will find a way to return said creatures from graveyard back to the battlefield. Ways they do this include simple spells that put creatures back onto the battlefield or into hand, creature or enchantments that have a triggered condition to bring the creatures back, or creatures or spells that will have you or opponents either discard or kill their own creatures and instantly return them from graveyard back to battlefield. Keywords that work with Reanimator include such things as

  • Undying, which states that when this creature dies, return it to the battlefield with a +1 +1 counter if it doesn't have one already on it
  • Persist, exactly like Undying but instead it works with the -1 -1 counters
  • Flashback, allows creatures or spells to be played from the graveyard for an alternate cost
  • Mill, sends cards from the top of the library directly to the graveyard
  • Dredge, allows players to return a specific card from graveyard back to hand by milling cards equal to its dredge in place of drawing a card(Dredge 3 would mean instead of drawing a card, you may put 3 cards from the top of your library to the graveyard to return said card from graveyard back to hand)
  • Decay, sometimes returning a creature back to battlefield comes with a drawback, Decay makes it so that the creature can no longer block and is sacrificed after combat if you attack with it

Generally Strategies for reanimator include, cards that return other cards and creatures from graveyard to hand, stack, or battlefield. In commander this can include your commander being your main reanimator engine, otherwise you'll want a few different unique cards to reanimate. A thing reanimator can do that other decks absolutly cannot, is that reanimator can remove a bit of card draw for milling due to the graveyard being another hand thats just slightly harder for the reanimator deck to use

Control

Control is an archtype that mainly focuses on what your opponent is doing or more or less what they are allowed to do. Control is mainly used in commanders/decks that are in the colors of blue and/or white. Control comes about most of the time in one of two ways, which corrospond to each of its colors. Blue is usually used for stopping someone when they attempt to do something. This mainly comes in the form of bouncing and countering. Bouncing is for permenants and its where that permenant is on the battlefield and is returned back into its owners hand. Countering is used for any spells on the stack(which include those permenants before they hit the battlefield. White is usually reserved for complete denial. These include the many abilites and spells that just say can't. The other control that white does is exile effects. Exile is where the permenant card is removed from the battlefield but is placed in exile instead of the graveyard. Something placed in exile is extremly hard to interact with or get back unless dictated by said removal card. Keywords that are used in this archetype include

  • Ward, usually coming with either a number or something specific to do, means that when an opponent targets said warded permenant they must pay or do the cost otherwise the ability or spell is countered.
  • Hexproof and Shroud, Hexproof is where opponents cannot target the hexproofed permenant while shroud is all players cannot target said permenant.
  • Protection, very confusing but can be remembered by the word DEBT, protection from a color means D- cannot be damaged by any spells or creatures of said color. E- cannot be enchanted or equipped by any enchantments or artifacts of said color. B - Creatures of the protected color cannot block your protected creature. T- The permenant cannot be targeted by any spells or abilites of said color.
  • Flash, allows something(usually a permenant) that is cast at sorcery speed to be casted at instant speed. Basically, something you'd usually only be able to play on the main phase of your turn could instead be cast as a response on anyone's turn.

General Strategies for control decks include creatures that can be casted at instant speed and usually with a counter effect on them, with the rest of the deck having counter and control spells. Another way to play control is some creatures your opponents have a hard time interacting with as well as those counter spells. These gameplans are slow but methodical as you send out creatures to wittle down your opponents life while stopping anything your opponent tries to play with your counter spells. These type of decks need a lot of card draw as to have enough counterspells to stop the opponent from dropping anything big that could out damage your few small creatures.

Combo

Combo's archetype is simplified to just 2 or more cards that can do something infinitly to further the game or outright win the game. All colors can use this archtype. Generally these combos fall into a few types

  • Infinite mana, as you can probably guess infinite mana allows the player to cast any spell that want and usually if the gameplan is to have infinite mana they will use it in a spell or ability that has X in its cost to do it for an infinite amount, or a simple ability that deals damage to deal infinite instances of damage to their opponents to win the game
  • Instant win, This either works with specific cards that say you win the game and instantly completes the requirment to win the game
  • Infinite action, this usually has cards that lets you deal damage whenever you do a specific action and then you have something that lets you do that action infinitly.

General Strategies include many tutors which allows you to take the infinite combo cards from your library and put them directly into hand or battlefield. Another strategy is just a lot of card draw to put a lot of your deck into hand which is more rng based compared to the tutors. These decks are very hit or miss as you either get the combo or you don't and if your gameplan is just the combo, there is a chance a control player stops your combo so you need ways to deny that or to bring back your combo.

Aggro

Aggro is an archtype that wants to win as fast as possible by putting as many creatures down and just send them swinging. Colors used in this archetype is mainly red with some decks being in white. Red and white will use spells and abilites to create many small creatures and use enchantments or abilites to buff up their creatures for a turn to try to take out opponents quickly. Aggro pairs well with Tribal due to the fact that some tribal will double tokens depending on the amount of a certain creature type you have as well as having more permenant and bigger buffs to said tokens. Keywords in Aggro

  • Haste, allows creatures to attack and use tap abilites the same turn they're played
  • Battlecry, whenever this creature attacks, it increases each other attacking creatures power by 1 until end of turn
  • Prowess, increases this creatures power and toughness by 1 for each non-creature spell you've cast this turn
  • Riot, creature enters the battlefield with Haste or a +1 +1 counter

General Strategies for Aggro include low cost creatures that create either more creatures, or temporary buffed creatures, or buff, all their or only this creature/s. Aggro pairs well with other Strategies like Tribal, Combo, etc. as many of those strategies use a few cards to dominate the game while Aggro just wants to win as fast as possible so as combo will use an infinite combo to generate infinite mana, a aggro player might put a low cost combo in the deck as both cards already synergize with the deck but win if you draw them together.

Gimmik

Gimmik archetype or basicaly meme or joke decks are decks that center around a one trick pony strategy. This includes unique cards or trying to achieve a weird board state like never being able to lose because a card says so but said card has no types making it impossible to interact with. Gimmiks require a lot of rng and set up to pull off but are very enjoyable when pulled off. There are thousands of Magic the Gathering cards with over 200 sets. Some cards had set exclusive keywords or abilites while others were mechanically unique for a reason. You just kind of have to find one of those cards and determine a game plan as Magic the Gathering isn't a solo experience and having fun is always the end goal so having a game plan that isn't about winning is totally acceptable. Just remember that your cards are Magic the Gathering and not solitare so don't go around trying to cause 100 turn games just to get the Gimmik to work!

Tribal

Tribal is the archetype which focuses on a specific creature type. They use the many different spells and permenants that include said creature type and either play specificly to the creatures unique abilites or try to get as many big or as many creatures of that type onto the battlefield. Tribal mainly includes anything that references the creature type, this includes many cards that just create tokens of said creature type or even ones that reference multiple creature types. The biggest of these tribes are

  • Humans, this is one of the main white Tribal types which has more silencing effects on their creatures while creating a bunch of tokens
  • Goblins, due mainly to Krinko whoc doubles your goblins each time you can activate his abilities and due to their low costs, Goblins are a mainly red tribe
  • Elves, are the main green tribe, they specalize in a lot of their creatures being mana dorks(they generate mana) as well as lords(they buff other creatures of their type)
  • Vampires, are the main black tribe, they have the most lords of any other creatures as well as regular black shenanagins that give vampires buffs whenever something dies
  • Merfolk, are the main blue tribe, these mermaids usually have some sort of control added onto them like islandwalk(if the defending player has an island, this creature cannot be blocked) or counters as their abilies
  • Artifacts, being the main colorless tribe, as well as being a card type is also technically a tribe as there are many colorless and color cards that buff or create colorless artifact creatures
  • Slivers, are the main WUBURG or all color tribe and main tribal type in general as each sliver will buff all slivers somehow, either giving them generic keyword buffs or allowing them to have unique abilites

General Strategies include making a bunch of tokens as in for Humans or Goblins, Having a bunch of Lords like Elves and Vampires, other strategies for specific tribes like merfolk and slivers, or with enough support just have a full deck of stuff that helps your tribe like with artifact Tribal
